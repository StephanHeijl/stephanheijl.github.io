# AI connectivity consulting landing page — design

## Purpose

Add a dedicated, ad-ready page for owners of small software products and practical service businesses. It should make a concrete offer: help their existing software, documents, and operational data work usefully with AI clients. The page is for people who already use free ChatGPT to think through work, but who do not want an abstract "AI strategy" engagement.

## Page shape

The page lives at `/ai-connectivity.html` and uses the site's existing type and visual language, with page-specific styles isolated under a new body class. A compact header keeps the name and the primary conversion action visible without sending ad visitors into the rest of the portfolio. The hero leads with a plain promise—make the software people already pay for usable from an AI conversation—followed by a short qualification: this is for a real workflow, not an AI demo.

Three outcome blocks explain the work in everyday terms: finding the right information, taking carefully limited actions in existing systems, and leaving behind something the client can run. A short four-step process makes the engagement feel bounded: choose a useful workflow, map the data and permissions, build and test the connection, then hand it over. A final section names suitable starting points for both software founders and service-business owners, plus what a first conversation covers.

## Conversion and tone

The main action is a LinkedIn message link with campaign parameters so ad traffic can be distinguished. Copy stays specific, brief, and honest about the work: no claims of transformation, no vague promises of seamlessness, and no generic AI language. It names systems, permissions, customer questions, and handover instead of using hype.

## Responsiveness and quality bar

The layout is single-column and readable by default, expanding into small grids only when space permits. The page must remain usable at 375px, 768px, 1024px, and 1440px widths, with no horizontal overflow, clipped calls to action, or overlapping sticky header. Automated source-level tests cover the page contract; browser checks cover layout at each breakpoint.
